01 / ABOUT

I’ve always been curious about
why people do what they do.

That question is the thread through almost everything I’ve studied. In psychology, it meant looking at behavior, development, decision-making, and the difference between what people say and what the evidence actually shows. In marketing and advertising, it meant asking who a message is for, why it should matter to them, and what would make them respond.

My research experience made me comfortable with both sides of analysis. Quantitative work taught me to look for patterns in measurable outcomes: changes, rates, relationships, and trends. Qualitative work taught me not to lose the context behind those numbers: language, motivation, perception, and the reasons a behavior may be happening in the first place.

I like having both. A number can tell you that something changed. Qualitative context can help explain why. That is the way I naturally approach digital work too. I want to know what performed, but I also want to know what the audience was seeing, what the message sounded like, what else was happening culturally, and whether the creative actually fit the platform.

02 / DIGITAL IN PRACTICE

I treat social media like
a live research environment.

OBSERVE

Read the Audience

I watch what people are responding to before I decide what I want to make. That includes trends, sounds, editing styles, humor, pacing, hooks, comments, repost behavior, visual choices, and the way different communities use the same platform differently.

TEST

Put the Idea Out There

Once something is published, I stop treating it like a precious idea and start treating it like information. Did people stay? Did they engage? Did the hook work? Did the format feel natural? Was the timing right? The response becomes part of the next decision.

ANALYZE

Look Beyond the Vanity Metric

I care about reach and likes, but I also think about what they mean. Strong performance without the right audience can still be the wrong result. I look at engagement, audience response, context, and the creative choices that may have contributed to the outcome.

ADJUST

Make the Next Version Better

I’m comfortable changing the idea when the audience gives me a reason to. That might mean changing the first few seconds, reframing the caption, simplifying the message, shifting the visual, or leaving a trend alone because it no longer fits.

03 / HOW I ANALYZE

Numbers first.
Context right beside them.

I don’t think of analysis as making a chart and calling it a day. I think of it as building a case. What happened? How confident are we that it matters? What other explanation could there be? What information is missing? And what decision can we actually make from what we know?

My psychology and behavioral research work trained me to work with qualitative and quantitative findings, design research methods, build data-collection procedures, review literature, and identify behavioral patterns. That background is useful in digital work because campaign data can be noisy. A spike can be meaningful, or it can be timing. A strong click-through rate can be encouraging, or it can hide a weak conversion. Good analysis means knowing what question you are trying to answer before you decide which number matters.

That is also why I enjoy trend analysis. Trends are not just whatever happens to be popular that week. I look at who is using a format, how quickly it is moving, what meaning the audience has attached to it, whether it fits the brand or message, and whether joining it would feel timely or late.
