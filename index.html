01 / ABOUT

My subject has always
been people.

I studied Psychology and Dance Performance at Southern Methodist University, with minors in Marketing and Advertising. Those subjects gave me different ways to study the same thing: what people notice, what they feel, what they remember, and what moves them to act.

Psychology and behavioral research trained me to look for patterns instead of assumptions. Marketing and advertising taught me to think about positioning, audience, message, and response. Performance gave me a much more immediate education in attention. You learn quickly that an audience does not owe you interest. You have to understand the room, make choices with intention, and adjust when something is not connecting.

I’ve carried that thinking into social media. My platforms became a place to study culture and behavior in real time: emerging formats, sounds, hooks, visual language, posting patterns, audience reactions, and the difference between content that is technically good and content that actually lands.

02 / DIGITAL IN PRACTICE

Audience behavior isn’t
abstract to me.

AUDIENCE

Built, Not Borrowed

I’ve grown an Instagram audience to nearly 10,000 followers and generated more than 1.1 million likes on TikTok. Those numbers came from repeatedly putting creative work in front of real audiences, seeing what they responded to, and learning from the result.

TRENDS

Studying the Feed

I pay attention to how social language changes: formats, sounds, editing styles, humor, hooks, pacing, captions, visual choices, and the point where a trend shifts from emerging to oversaturated. I use trends as signals, not instructions.

TESTING

Learning From Response

Publishing content creates a constant feedback loop. I look at what earns attention, what gets ignored, what people share or react to, and how presentation changes response. When something misses, I’m comfortable changing the approach.

CREATIVE

Platform-Aware Judgment

What works on TikTok does not automatically work on Instagram. I think about the platform, the audience, the first impression, the message, and the action I want someone to take before deciding how an idea should be presented.

03 / WHY THAT MATTERS

I’ve worked on both sides
of the insight.

My research background taught me how to study behavior in a structured setting. My marketing and advertising education taught me how organizations think about audiences. Building my own social presence taught me what those ideas feel like when the audience can respond immediately.

That combination matters to me because digital work is not only about producing content or pulling a report. The useful question is what happens next. Why did one message outperform another? Is the audience changing? Is the creative wrong for the channel? Is a trend actually relevant to the people we are trying to reach? What can the numbers tell us before the next post, ad, email, or campaign goes live?

I’m comfortable moving between those questions. I can look at performance analytically without forgetting that there is a person on the other side of the metric, and I can approach creative work with instinct without treating instinct as evidence.
