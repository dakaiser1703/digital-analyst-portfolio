# Portfolio Writing Guide

Use this guide whenever editing the site copy.

## Keep it human

- Write like a smart person explaining their work to another person, not like a corporate brochure.
- Prefer concrete verbs and plain English.
- Vary sentence length naturally.
- Keep claims proportional to the actual experience behind them.
- Let specific details do the convincing instead of calling everything strategic, innovative, impactful, dynamic, or data-driven.
- Avoid generic openings and conclusions that could belong to anyone's portfolio.
- Avoid repetitive three-part lists just for rhythm.
- Avoid unnecessary em dashes, inflated metaphors, and over-explaining.
- Do not use fake quotations or invented metrics.
- Do not pretend familiarity with tools or platforms that is not supported by the resume or actual work.
- Keep headings short and conversational.
- Read every page once specifically for AI-sounding phrasing and rewrite anything that feels too polished, symmetrical, promotional, or generic.

## Voice

Curious, capable, creative, analytical, warm, and early-career. Confident without pretending to know everything.

## Site focus

The portfolio should emphasize Darius Kaiser's bio, real resume experience, skills, education, and contact information. The design and code of the website can demonstrate digital fluency without invented case studies.
